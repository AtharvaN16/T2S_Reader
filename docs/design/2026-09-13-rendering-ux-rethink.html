Rendering: where the flow drops the reader

Chapter rendering works. What it doesn't do is tell you it is working, let you stop it, or show you what it produced — once the Book sheet closes, every trace of an app-wide queue disappears.

01 / THE FLOW TODAY

Four ways in, one place to look, no way out

The queue (ChapterRenderRunner) is app-wide and drains one chapter at a time. Its entire visible surface is a mark on a chapter row inside one book's render mode.

ENTRY WHAT YOU SEE Home ⋯ → "Render chapter" ───┐ nothing at all Reader ⋯ → "Render this chapter" ───┤ nothing at all Collection ⋯ → "Render chapter" ───┤ opens Book sheet in render mode Book sheet ⋯ → "Render chapters" ───┤ render mode │ ▼ ┌───────────────────────┐ │ ChapterRenderRunner │ one queue, whole app │ queued│running│ready │ serial, one at a time │ │failed │ not persisted across launches └───────────┬───────────┘ │ ┌──────────────────┴──────────────────┐ ▼ ▼ Book sheet render mode one toast, 4 seconds, per-row: Queued / waveform / when the WHOLE queue drains tick+size+trash / warning miss it and it is gone ↑ close the sheet and this is gone cancel(jobID) and cancelAll() exist on the runner, are tested — and no view calls them.

The 2026-09-12 design says this out loud: “No permanent indicator: the sheet is where this lives.” That was a deliberate simplification, not an oversight — which is why fixing it is a design decision to make, not a bug to patch.

02 / WHERE IT FAILS

Eight specific breaks

#What breaksWhy it happens
1Two entry points are silent. Home ⋯ and Reader ⋯ enqueue into a queue with no visible target. You cannot tell it worked.Feedback lives in the Book sheet; these two never open it.
2Progress is attached to the wrong object. The queue is app-wide, but its only display belongs to one book's sheet. Render chapters from two books and no screen shows both.Render mode was designed as a picker, and the marks came along for the ride.
3No stop, no pause. A 20-chapter queue cannot be cancelled at all.cancel()/cancelAll() are written and tested but unwired.
4Completion is a 4-second toast. Backgrounded, reading, or screen-off and you never learn it finished — or that 2 of 5 failed.lastCompletion is retained but nothing renders it after the toast.
5"Ready to play" is invisible where you play from. Home rows and Collection tiles say nothing about what is on the device.Only BookAudioStatus knows, and only the sheet reads it.
6A relaunch silently loses the queue. Chapters queued but not yet run simply never happen.Explicitly out of scope in the design; nothing tells the reader.
7Evict has three shapes in three places. Row trash · "Evict all" pill · Settings → Storage "Evict" pill + bar + %.Grown in two passes, never unified.
8Delete looks calmer than Evict. Evict (recoverable — re-render it) is red and loud. Delete (removes the book) is a quiet menu item next to it.Severity is inverted relative to consequence.
03 / THE PERSISTENT INDICATOR

Where does “rendering is happening” live?

Three options. All of them assume the same two model changes: wire up cancelAll(), and keep the last completion until it is seen rather than for four seconds.

04 / SPACE, EVICT, DELETE

One verb, three shapes — and the severities are backwards

Today: a trash glyph on a chapter row, an “Evict all” pill in the sheet header, and an “Evict” pill with a bar and a percentage in Settings → Storage. Delete-the-book sits in the same ⋯ as Evict all, styled quieter than the thing it outranks.

05 / WHAT I'D BUILD

The shortlist

A + C for the indicator, 1 for storage — in that order of value:

  • The render strip + queue sheet (A). Fixes failures 1, 2, 3 and 4 at once, and wires two methods that are already written and tested. Biggest return, smallest new surface.
  • Storage → “On this device” (1), with the eviction wording. Fixes 7 and 8, and gives the page the one number it is missing.
  • State chips on books (C). Fixes 5. Cheap, but only worth doing after A — on its own it is decoration without control.
  • Persisting the queue is the honest fix for 6 and the only one that touches the model rather than the views. Worth its own decision, and probably its own pass.
