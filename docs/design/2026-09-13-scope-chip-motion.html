One bar, one chip, held still

The status line lifts clear of the controls. The bar becomes a single unbroken chapter scrubber across the full width. The chip stays dead centre no matter what the clocks say.

Fixed since revision 1 the growing segment now actually reaches full width, and the chip no longer drifts when the clocks change width.
Settled tinted status line, lifted · twin-bars chip, centred on the row · spring on both.
01

The status line, lifted

Tinted only — glow at meta size, no capsule. The whole point of moving it is separation, so the gap is the design. Drag the slider: at the bottom of its range it is a caption stuck to the chapter title; by about 90 pt it has left the controls behind and reads as a note about the page instead of a label on the transport.

Keep it centred on the page, not between anything. This is the same trap the clock row hit: two Spacer()s centre a word between its neighbours, not on the row. Floating free above the chapter title there are no neighbours, so a plain .frame(maxWidth: .infinity) is correct — but it must be an overlay on the reading area, not a row in the bottom VStack, or it will push the transport down every time it appears.
02

The morph, working

Tap the chip or the bar. The current chapter's segment travels to left: 0 and width: 100% while every other segment fades out beneath it — one unbroken rounded bar across the whole width, ticked at chapter resolution, with the clocks rescoped to the chapter.

Why revision 1 didn't grow. Each segment carried its share as an inline flex-grow, and an inline style outranks any stylesheet rule — so the chapter rule that was supposed to take it to full width never applied. Worth knowing because the SwiftUI equivalent has the same shape: ThinScrubber.layout(width:) computes shares from spans and only then applies activeShare. Drive the scope through that same clamp and there is one place where width is decided; compute it anywhere else and the two paths will disagree exactly like this did.
Still true: the current chapter has to be ticked at chapter resolution in both scopes. Here it carries 40 ticks squeezed into 10% of the bar at rest, which costs nothing and means the tick grid never re-lays-out mid-spring. Slice the book-wide 48 instead and the fill flickers as it grows.
03

The chip holds its ground

Left: centred with two Spacer()s — it sits midway between the clocks, so it lurches sideways the moment -21:37:14 becomes -46:20. Right: an overlay centred on the row itself, which cannot move. Toggle both and watch only the left one jump.

05

Notes for the build

  • ScrubberModel grows a scope. Ticks are a book-wide pass today. The current chapter needs its own tick pass at chapter resolution, available in both scopes.
  • The clocks change meaning, so elapsedText can't be reused as-is. Chapter scope needs elapsed − chapterStart and chapterEnd − elapsed. ChapterEntry.axis already has the spans; nothing new has to be computed.
  • Scrubbing in chapter scope. With the bar at full width the existing gesture already maps the finger to this chapter alone. The chapter picker above stays the only way to reach a different one — decide whether dragging to either end nudges into the neighbour.
  • The chip must not change the row's height. Run it at 18 pt inside a clock row that keeps its current height, or the transport shifts down by six points.
  • Haptic. .selection on the chip. Nothing on the bar — it already fires while scrubbing.
  • Accessibility. accessibilityValue must name the scope: "42 percent of chapter 2". The chip is a two-option picker, not a button.
