01

Two things to settle before the wording

State or destination? A single chip that flips can either name where you are or name where tapping takes you — and the two read identically while saying opposite things. This is the oldest bug in toggle design, and it is unfixable by better wording: the only real cures are showing both options at once, or making the chip's own appearance change so obviously that no one has to read it as a sentence. Cards A and C below are the same chip under the two readings, so you can feel the difference.

The centre of the clock row is already occupied. ReaderPage.swift overlays the status line — preparing the voice…, catching up… — across the whole clock row, deliberately centred on the row rather than between the clocks. A centred chip collides with it. So the chip goes left of the elapsed clock or right of the remaining clock, not in the middle.

Redundancy watch. The chapter row directly above already reads "Chp 2: Believers and Infidels". A chip that says Chapter repeats a word that is six points away and twice the size. That is an argument for the glyph families, or for wording that names something the row above does not — a count, a span, a shape.
02

Placement

Same chip, three homes. The clock row is 13 pt meta in ink2; a 22 pt pill sets the row height, which nudges the transport down by about six points unless the row is allowed to keep its height and the chip runs at 18.

05

What I'd build

A — the twin-bars two-up control, right-aligned in the clock row. Two 18 pt glyph cells in a 11 pt-radius trough: on the left, four short bars with gaps; on the right, one unbroken rounded bar. The lit one is the scope you are in. It is not an icon standing for the idea of a book — it is a miniature of the bar it switches to, six points under the bar itself, so the pairing is read rather than learned.

It answers your objection directly: it never says Book, or any word at all, so it cannot repeat the chapter title above it or make anyone wonder whether "Book" is where they are or where they're going. Both states are visible, so the state-versus-action trap closes. And it is the one option whose meaning improves rather than decays when the bar beside it changes shape.

If you want a single chip instead of two cells, take J — 2 / 14 over any of the word pairs. It states a fact rather than issuing a label, so the state/action ambiguity mostly dissolves; in book scope it reads 14 chapters and in chapter scope 2 / 14. It is the only worded option that tells you something the row above doesn't.

Cost either way: the accessibility label has to carry what the glyph won't — "Scrubber scope, whole book" / "Scrubber scope, chapter 2 of 14" — and it needs a real accessibilityValue on the scrubber itself, since "42 percent" means two different things depending on this control.

06

Still open

  • Does the chip animate the bar between scopes, or hard-cut? The bar already springs at 0.25 s when a chapter widens under the finger — reusing that curve to grow the current chapter out to full width would make the two views obviously the same bar.
  • Chapterless books (PDF, plain text) draw one unbroken bar already. The chip should hide, not disable — there is nothing to switch.
  • One chapter only? Same answer as above: hide.
  • Left or right of the clocks. Right sits under the render frontier's far end and next to the number that changes most; left sits under the played portion. Section 02 has both.
  • Dynamic Type. At the larger accessibility sizes a 22 pt pill and two clocks will not share a line. Does the chip wrap to its own row, or drop to the glyph-only form?
