Book bar, chapter bar

Six ways to let the scrubber change scope — and an argument for which one the app should take. Every mock below is live: click the highlighted target to see the switch.

Today one segmented bar spans the whole book — one flat bar per chapter, 3 pt gaps, 48 ticks carrying the render frontier.
Wanted an option to scope that bar to the current chapter instead.
Open what the control is, and where it lives.
01

What the two bars actually are

The difference is smaller than it sounds on the bar and bigger than it sounds in the clocks. Scoping to the chapter turns a 24-hour bar into a 2½-hour one: the same 6 pt of pixels now spans a tenth of what it did, so a thumb-width finally means something. But the loudest change is underneath — 2:43:40 / -21:37:14 becomes 1:43:40 / -46:20, which is the number people are usually after when they reach for this.

A consequence worth pricing now: ticks are computed book-wide in ScrubberModel.renderedTicks. Zoomed to one chapter you would be stretching maybe two of the 48 across the whole bar — a smear, not a frontier. Chapter scope needs its own tick pass over that chapter's utterances, which means ScrubberModel grows a scope, not just ThinScrubber.

04

What I'd build

A, with D behind it. Tapping the clock row flips scope, and the choice persists in preferences so it survives the sheet closing — with the same setting exposed in Appearance → Reading for anyone who never finds the tap. That is one gesture, one stored preference, and zero pixels added to the player.

The reason to put the control on the clocks rather than anywhere else is that the clocks are the payoff. The bar already solves precision scrubbing inside a chapter — pressing it widens the active chapter to 45% and maps the finger's travel onto that chapter alone. What chapter scope actually adds is the answer to "how long is left in this chapter", and that answer is a number, printed in the row you are about to tap. Control and effect are the same object.

Keep the scope word on the right of that row permanently, in ink2 at meta size: it is the only discoverability affordance A has, and without it the feature is a secret.

E is the interesting rival and worth a cheap spike before committing, because it deletes the mode entirely. If holding the existing zoom for a few seconds after release turns out to feel good rather than twitchy, the whole preference disappears and nothing needs a home. The risk is purely feel, which means it's a build-and-try, not an argue.

05

Open questions

  • Bookmarks. Today the dots draw only while pressed, and only for the chapter under the finger, because a dulled 11 pt bar would smear them. In chapter scope there is room — should they be permanently visible there?
  • Sleep timer. With a sleep timer running, is the right-hand number the chapter's remainder or the timer's? Chapter scope makes that collision more likely, not less.
  • Books with no chapters (PDF, plain text) already draw one unbroken bar. Does the control hide entirely, or show disabled?
  • Does scope persist per book or globally? Per book is more correct and more state; global is simpler and probably what anyone expects.
  • VoiceOver. The scrubber is one element with a percent value. Scope changes what that percent means, so the accessibility value needs the scope in it — "42 percent of chapter 2" rather than "42 percent".
