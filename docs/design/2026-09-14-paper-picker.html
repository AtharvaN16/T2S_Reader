Choosing a paper

“How many colours are there, four?” — the swatch is answering a question nobody asked. Six ways to ask the real one, drawn at the size they would be in the sheet.

The problem a split chip with two inks and a name under it is four things per swatch, sixteen times over. The eye counts regions before it counts papers.
The thing we keep forgetting this sheet opens over the Reader, at a medium detent, with the page still on screen behind it. The truest preview of a paper is the page itself, already there, updating as you tap. The swatch does not have to preview anything — it only has to be identifiable.
Each option below is drawn twice: on Sepia in daylight and on Sepia at night, because the picker wears the paper it is choosing.

The one problem none of them solves by itself

In dark mode eight zen papers are eight near-blacks. Sepia is #17120C, Ink is #101010, Rose is #1A0F10 — as 44 pt dots they are the same dot. The difference between dark papers lives in the ink: cream, white, pink-white. Any picker that shows only the page will be unreadable at night, which is exactly when a reader goes looking for it.

Which is why the recommendation is C. The letter is not decoration: at night it is the only part of the swatch carrying the difference. B is the calmest picker here and the one I would have chosen if the papers were all light — in the dark it is a row of black dots.

03

What I would build

C, with the name in the header. One face — the one the phone is actually in — a letter in that face's ink, no name under each swatch, and the chosen paper's name where the section header is: “Paper · Sepia”. Sixteen swatches become sixteen objects instead of sixty-four.

Two rows, still labelled. Zen and Pop are a real distinction and the labels are cheap: “Quiet” and “Loud” say it in one word each, better than “Paper” and “Paper · loud” do now.

And the page is the preview. The sheet already sits at a medium detent over the Reader, so half the book is visible while you choose. Tapping a swatch repaints it instantly — that is what makes the small swatch enough. If it feels short of confirmation, the cheapest addition is a line of the book's own text inside the sheet, in the chosen paper, above the rows.
