Sixteen papers: eight zen, eight pop

Eight quiet papers to read a book on, eight loud ones to read a chapter on. They colour the Reader page and nothing else — Home, the Collection and Settings keep the app’s own greys — and every one of them has a lit and an unlit face, because light and dark stay.

Asked for 2026-09-14 — “8 pastel and zen colors and 8 poppy bright bold colors … this will only apply to the reader and not throughout the app. The light and dark mode are kept.”
How to read a card the Reader as it would be: the book’s title bar, a sentence already read, the sentence being read in that paper’s wash, the text ahead dimmed, the scrubber and the transport. Left is the theme in light mode, right the same theme in dark.
Not built yet. Say which of the sixteen survive and I build those.
01

The picker

Two rows, because they are two different things to want. Each swatch is split — the paper’s lit face and its unlit one — since a theme is a hue now and the phone still decides whether it is day or night. Tapping one sets the paper; the app around the Reader does not change.

02

Zen

Paper tints: enough hue to see across a whole screen of it, not enough to argue with anything drawn on top. These keep the app’s own colours — the blue key, the orange playhead — because a tinted page can host them.

03

Pop

The page is the colour. Which means the page can no longer host four more: a blue key on a cobalt page is an object hiding inside its own background, and a red delete on cherry is invisible. So a pop paper takes its chrome from itself.

THE POP RULE
On a pop paper the Reader’s chrome is drawn in that paper’s own ink and paper — nothing else. The key is ink-filled with paper-coloured lettering, the scrubber is ink at three opacities, the read-along wash is the ink at 12%. One colour on the screen, and it is the one you chose. Zen papers keep the app’s palette; pop papers borrow nothing.
04

What the rule buys, on one page

05

The numbers

Ink on paper, for every face of every theme. 7.0+ is what a full screen of prose wants, 4.5–7.0 is comfortable but not a novel, under 4.5 would not ship.

Every zen paper clears 13:1 — they are papers, and ink is ink. The pop papers land between 6.4 and 21: Cherry, Cobalt and Violet are the low three, and they are low because they are the vivid ones, which is the whole point of the family. They were each lightened until pure-black ink cleared 7, so they read as posters rather than as a dare. If a chapter on Cherry turns out to be a headache, it is the paper to drop first.

06

Building it

The Reader only. A ReaderPaper resolves seven values — page, ink, dimmed, hairline, surface, wash, mark — from the chosen theme and the current light/dark trait. Only the Reader page and its own chrome read it; Tokens is untouched, so no other screen can be surprised by it.

Light and dark stay. The app-wide theme keeps System · Light · Dark and keeps governing everything else. A paper has two faces and the trait picks one, which is why the swatches are split: choosing Sky does not mean choosing day.

Sixteen is a lot of swatches. Two rows of eight at 58 pt fits a sheet without scrolling, and the split chip means no row has to be shown twice. If it reads as busy, the honest cut is Zen 8 + Pop 6 (drop Mono, which is what Paper already is, and one of the three low ones).

Cost. The paper table and the resolver, the picker, the pop rule in the Reader’s chrome, and a screenshot of each face — call it a day, and it ends with sixteen photographs to choose from for real.
