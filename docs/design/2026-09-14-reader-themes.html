Paper, ink, and the four colours that must survive them

A reader theme colours the page you read on. It must not colour the four things the app uses to mean something — progress, the key that starts something, a delete, a success — or the page stops being able to say them.

Asked for 2026-09-14 — “show the theme as a swatch … we can have multiple different themes, from bright red to pastel red, blue … think of the swatch option, and edge cases (so the reader theme does not clash with any UI elements)”.
Decides the palette, the swatch control, what happens to System, and the one rule that keeps a theme from eating the app.
Not built yet. Nothing here is in the app: pick the papers you want and I will build exactly those.
01

The rule: paper is a tint, not a colour

The Reader draws four colours that carry meaning, and all four have to keep working on every page: accent is progress and the read-along, glow is the key that starts something, destructive is a delete, positive is a thing that finished. A saturated page competes with all of them at once — and a red page competes with the delete specifically, which is the one clash that is not merely ugly.

RULE A
Light papers stay under 8% saturation, dark papers under 18%. That is enough to see the hue on a whole screen of it and not enough to argue with a saturated glyph sitting on it. It is the difference between “pastel red” and “bright red”, and §3 shows what happens when you cross it.
RULE B
Ink is the paper’s own hue, darkened — never pure black. Black text on tinted paper reads as a sticker on the page; ink at the same hue reads as printing.
RULE C
Each theme pins its own read-along tint, at least 60° of hue from its paper. The read-along is an 8%-alpha wash — on paper of its own hue it simply disappears. Amber on blue paper, blue on red paper, and so on. The app already has this dial (HighlightTheme); the theme sets it rather than the reader.
RULE D
The four meaning-colours are per-theme values, not constants. On a wine paper the delete red is lifted until it clears the page by 3:1; on sky the key stays the same electric blue, because a saturated blue on an 6% blue tint still reads as an object. The theme owns the shift, so no screen has to know.
02

The swatch

A row of papers, each showing its own ink on itself — a letter, not an abstract square, because what you are choosing is “what does text look like on this”. Selected takes the app’s ring. Two rows, light and dark, and that is what replaces System: a theme is now a paper, and a paper is either lit or unlit. (See §6 for what happens to readers who are on System today.)

03

Bright red, pastel red

The owner asked for the range. Here it is, with the app’s own chrome laid on both.

On the bright paper the delete pill has to become white-on-red to be seen at all — at which point it is the same object as the page, and “delete” has stopped being a colour and become a shape. The read-along wash is gone. Body text at 4.9:1 is under the 7:1 a full screen of prose wants. The pastel keeps every one of them and still reads, unmistakably, as a red page.

04

The papers

Five lit, five unlit. Each card is the Reader as it would look: the chapter title, a sentence already read, the sentence being read now in that theme’s wash, the text ahead dimmed — then the chrome strip underneath, where the four meaning-colours sit on that paper, and the numbers.

05

The clash matrix

Every paper against every meaning-colour, as a contrast ratio. 3.0+ is the floor for a glyph or a pill, under 3.0 needs the per-theme lift of Rule D, under 2.0 cannot be rescued and the paper is wrong.

The amber column is a finding, not a slip. The accent orange scores 2.2–2.5 on every light paper and the green 2.7–3.0 — and that is true of the app today, since Paper is the light page it already ships. As a 6 pt bar or a dot those are marks, not text, and 2.5 is survivable; the moment a theme wants either as a word (“Rendering 2 of 3”, a “ready” tag) it needs Rule D. The lifts that work on every light paper: accent #C85A0A (4.0 on Paper, 3.6 on Sepia), positive #1B8C4A (4.0 / 3.7). The dark papers need no lift at all — everything is 5.4 or better.

06

What building it costs

Tokens. Tokens resolves light/dark through one dynamic UIColor. A theme is a third axis: the same tokens, resolved from the chosen paper instead of the trait. That is one lookup table and one @Environment value — every screen already reads Tokens.ink, so no view changes.

Scope. The reader theme colours the Reader page. Whether it also colours Home, the Collection and Settings is the real question — the owner said “applies to the whole app” should go from the label, which points at the page-only answer. Page-only is also the safe one: sepia paper with the app’s own greys around it is a book in an app, where a sepia app is a skin.

Migration. A reader on System today gets Paper if the device is light and Ink if it is dark, once, at first launch after the update — then the choice is theirs and stops following the phone.

Cost. Roughly: the palette + token plumbing, the swatch control, the per-theme highlight and the Rule D lifts, plus a pass over the Reader’s own chrome (the scrubber, the warm-up glow, the raised key). Half a day, and the screenshots to prove each paper.
