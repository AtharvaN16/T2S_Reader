03 / WHAT THIS CHANGES UNDERNEATH

The one real mechanism change, and the two questions it raises

Prepare stops being a budget and becomes a selection. Today RenderPolicy tier 3 walks the continue-document then the Queue against one prepareBudgetSeconds. Under this design "Keep up with my reading" is the resume chapter of every document — a bound the policy already knows how to express (it is the `manual` tier's chapter bound, applied to many documents) — and "Only what I pick" is an explicit set. Neither needs hours.

Open questionMy call, unless you say otherwise
Does the hours budget survive at all? "Everything in my Queue" has no equivalent here — a reader who wants eight hours banked must now pick chapters by hand. Let it go. A "Keep up with my reading" that covers every book's current chapter is already more than three hours for most libraries, and picking is more honest than a number nobody can translate. If you want the middle ground, "Keep up with my reading" could mean the current chapter and the next one.
What happens when a picked chapter finishes rendering? Does it stay picked forever, or does the pick clear? The pick clears when the chapter is on the device — so the list is always "still to do", and a chapter deleted from Storage is simply no longer prepared. Otherwise the picks silently accumulate into a list nobody prunes.
Overnight, exactly. iOS grants background time when it wants to. Treat the window as a refusal, not a schedule: outside 11 PM–7 AM a granted task finishes immediately. The promise we can keep is "never while you're using it", which is the one worth making.
