00 / WHAT IS ACTUALLY WRONG

The screen is four unrelated facts in one column

Not a hierarchy problem alone. Every section is drawn by the same section(title:) helper with the same 40 pt gap, so a 1.18 GB download, a scheduling preference, a cache ceiling and a list of books all arrive at the same weight — and two rows of visually identical pills mean two completely different kinds of thing.

Q1 / DOES PREPARE ON CHARGE WORK?

Yes. It picks the book you were last listening to, then your Queue — and the hours are hours of listening, not hours of work

QuestionWhat the code does
Does it run?Two ways. A BGProcessingTask (com.t2s.reader.prepare) with requiresExternalPower = true, asked for 15 minutes out and re-armed after every run; and a foreground pass. So: overnight, on a charger, without you opening the app.
When does it refuse?PrepareRunner.isSafe — it must be charging, not thermally serious, not in Low Power Mode, and the store must not be full. Two more gates you can't see: the voice must not still be standing in on the hosted mirror, and a foreground warm-up must have built this install's compute plans (KokoroWarmUpRecord) — a background launch will not build them, so after every update the first night is skipped.
Which documents?RenderPolicy tier .prepare: the continue-document first (the one with the most recent lastPlayedAt), then the Queue in its own order, deduped. Each book is walked from its resume position, not from page one, and one shared budget drains across them in order.
Why hours?The budget is in playback seconds. "3 hours" means three hours of listening ready ahead of where you are. It is the right unit for the promise; it is the wrong unit to sit directly above a row of GB pills, with no line anywhere converting one to the other.

The design problem, then, is not the unit — it's that the screen never says any of this. It doesn't say what "prepared" is prepared for, doesn't name the books, doesn't say it only runs on a charger, and doesn't say what the hours cost in megabytes. → four directions

Q2 / IS THERE A CAP ON WHAT THE APP TAKES?

No. The cap covers rendered audio only — about a third of what the app is actually holding

→ four directions, from "make the cap mean the whole app" to "drop the cap and manage against the phone's free space instead".

Q3 / WHAT ARE COMPUTE PLANS?

Core ML's compiled model, built on your phone at first launch — which is exactly why the total beats the download

Voice model · 620.8 MBCompute plans · 561.6 MB
Where fromDownloaded over Wi-Fi from the manifest Built on the device by Core ML the first time the voice warms up
Where it lives<App Support>/… — the installed revision Library/Caches/<bundle id>/com.apple.e5rt.e5bundlecache
What it isThe weights and the compiled .mlmodelc The graph specialised for this chip — the scheduling Core ML would otherwise redo on every load
Cost to rebuildA 620 MB download, over Wi-Fi No download. About a minute of a core per stage, in the foreground
Why it's fragile— iOS keys plans to the install. Every build and every App Store update warms a fresh generation and keeps the old one — the 11 Pro reached 4.36 GB across one day of builds (2026-09-11) and filled. KokoroPlanCache.prepare now wipes stale generations at launch.

The thing worth noticing: those two halves have wildly different delete costs, and the screen offers one button for both. KokoroPlanCache.wipe already exists and is already called at launch — so "reclaim 561.6 MB, rebuilt free the next time you plug in" is a real offer the app can already make, and it is a far better first answer than "delete everything and re-download 620 MB".

On prominence: the voice model is drawn by the same section(title:) helper as the other three, so the single largest object on the phone is a 17 pt heading like the rest. → three directions

05 / WHERE I'D LAND

One recommendation per question, if you want a shortcut

  • Prepare → D, the Queue made visible. It is the only direction that answers "which document" by showing you, and the budget becomes a line drawn across a list you already understand.
  • Cap → A, one budget over the whole app, with the stacked bar above it. If that's too much surgery, B keeps today's mechanics and just stops lying about them.
  • Voice model → C, split the two halves. It is nearly free — KokoroPlanCache.wipe exists — and it turns the screen's scariest button into a cheap one.
  • Per document → C, rows that open the Book sheet. Storage stops duplicating an interaction that already has a better home, and gets a proper "biggest first" list instead.
