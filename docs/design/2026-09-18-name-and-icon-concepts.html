Name and icon concepts

Four icon directions drawn from the app's own motifs — the word lit as it is spoken, and the voice's light blooming at the crown — beside the placeholder. Then the same at home-screen size, and the welcome beat with candidate names in it.

1 · Icons at 220 pt

Today: the placeholder
Text lines and a play triangle. It is the mark every TTS app on the store already uses.
A · The lit word, dark
What has been read is ink, what is being read is the accent and glows, what is coming is dim. The Reader's page as a mark.
B · The lit word, paper
The same mark on the light ground. Rarer on a home screen than a dark tile, and it is the Reader's default page.
C · The light at the crown, dark
The voice's bloom from the welcome and the voice page, with the page falling away beneath it. Quieter, less literal.
D · The light at the crown, paper
C on the light ground. The warmest of the four at a glance; the least distinct from a weather or sunrise app.

4 · The name survey (App Store, 2026-09-18)

NameWhy it fitsOn the store todayVerdict
LectorLatin for reader; the person whose job is to read aloud to a room — the lector of a service, or the one who read novels to the cigar rollers of Havana and Tampa all day. A reading voice, not a narrator, which is the app's own line.Nothing of that exact name. A Linux desktop e-book reader on GitHub; Spanish-language "Lector de voz" apps (lector is the ordinary Spanish word for reader); Lectory and Lectern nearby.Clean. Recommended.
SoftspokenHow the voice reads, and what the beds under it are for. Warm, bedtime, the sleep ruler and the soundscapes.No app of that name; a music artist and an ElevenLabs voice tag.Clean. Ten letters; the welcome would drop to 52 pt.
PagelightThe light on the page — the icon, literally.No app of that exact name; Lightpage (an AI journal) is the same two words reversed, and reading-light utilities cluster near it.Usable; some confusion with Lightpage.
GloamingScots for twilight, the hour the app is for. Reads as a quiet word even to people who have not met it.Nothing; only "Gloam", a Korean beauty booking app.Clean.
EvensongThe evening service that is sung rather than said: a voice at the end of the day.Nothing; the word is on Apple Music, not the App Store.Clean. Carries a church note.
BookfellowThe one who reads with you. Warm, a little old-fashioned, plainly about books.Nothing of that name; Bookmate and BookBuddy are neighbours.Clean. Ten letters; 52 pt on the welcome.
EndpaperThe leaf that binds a book to its cover; a quiet, bookish word almost nobody has claimed.No iOS app; endpaper.app is an AI ghostwriting service on the web, and Endpaper Labs makes Android puzzles.Usable; the ghostwriting site is an awkward neighbour.
DulcetDulcet tones: sweet-sounding. The voice, in one word.A habit-breaking app of that name exists on social media; no clear store listing found.Probably free; check in App Store Connect.
NightjarThe bird that sings at dusk. Short, strange, memorable."The Nightjar", a 2013 audio game, was delisted years ago; the name may still be held by its developer.Usable if the name has been released.
LiltThe rise and fall of a pleasant voice: exactly what a good reading voice has. Four letters.No app of that name, but Lilt Inc. is an AI translation company with the word as its mark in software.Trademark risk, not store risk.
ToldA story, told. Four letters, calm."Told." — a predictions game — took the name in 2026; TELL (family stories) nearby.Same name, unrelated category. Apple may refuse the exact string.
