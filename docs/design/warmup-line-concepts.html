01 / THE PREMISE

If the time is the valuable part, it cannot be the part that rotates away

That is the whole argument of this version. A rotator that cycles “about 15 s → 120 of 350 MB → only this once” hides the time for two beats out of three — and the time is the one thing a waiting reader is actually looking for. So the line splits three ways instead of two:

The shape

Anchor — what is happening. Fixed, never moves.
Rotator — the detail and the reassurance. This is the only thing that animates.
Clock — the time. Fixed, always on screen, hung off the bar rather than sharing the sentence.

02 / TEXT

Seven ways the words change

All at 1:1, all on the same copy, all with the same 3.2 s dwell so they are comparable. Two problems each one either solves or does not: does the anchor stay put, and does the line re-centre with a snap. The line is centred and single-line, so a tail that changes width drags the anchor sideways — which is why T1 and T2 look calmer than they should and T3–T5 need a fix for it.

03 / TIME

Seven ways to show it

Bearing in mind what the number actually is: expectedWarmUpSeconds is how long the last warm-up took on this phone. It is a memory, not a measurement. A second-by-second countdown dresses a guess as a fact and then visibly runs out — which is why the code already falls back to “almost there”. Options M5 and M7 sidestep that by showing the guess as a guess.

04 / THE SEAM

Plain bar becomes segmented bar

Download is one continuous byte count, so it is a plain bar. The compile reports stage N of total and the warm-up reports eight stages, so those are segmented. Each frame below loops the moment between: plain filling → the change → segmented filling. The thing to avoid is already documented in the code — warmUpProgressFloor exists because the owner watched the bar slide backwards at exactly this seam.

05 / WHEN TO SEGMENT

06 / ASSEMBLED

The whole thing, end to end

One frame, looping the full first-launch sequence at real pace: waiting → downloading (plain, clock) → preparing (segments deal out) → warming (eight stages) → ready. Anchor fixed, rotator crossfading, clock never leaving. This is the recommendation, running.

Still the one that matters

A failed warm-up shows a green “Voice ready”. KokoroStatusModel.update calls beginReadyBeat() on any transition out of a warming state (KokoroComposition.swift:154), and .unavailable and .removed both have isWarming == false. So a download that failed, a stage load that failed twice, and a model the reader deleted in Storage all end on the green light and the words “Voice ready”. Then the glow fades and the book reads in the system voice with nothing said.

10 / IF IT WERE MINE

What I’d build

  • Bar: 88 × 5, as you picked. Plain during the download, segmented for the compile and the warm-up.
  • Seam: S3, the handoff — the full download bar shrinks in place to become the first of three, and the rest appear beside it. It is the only one where the download is visibly kept rather than replaced, which is what warmUpProgressFloor was written to protect.
  • Text: T4, reserved tail. The anchor is genuinely fixed because the rotator’s box is sized once to its widest string, so nothing re-centres and nothing snaps. T2’s crossfade motion on top of it.
  • Clock: M2, hung off the right edge of the bar with the bar itself still dead centre. Always there, never rotating, tabular so the digits don’t dance — and it degrades to “almost there” and then to nothing rather than counting into the negative.
  • Dwell rule: rotate only when the expected wait is over 12 s. An everyday launch shows one line, one clock, eight stages, and goes.
  • Before any of it: the three fix rows in §08. A green “Voice ready” over a failed warm-up is the only thing here a reader can actually be misled by.
