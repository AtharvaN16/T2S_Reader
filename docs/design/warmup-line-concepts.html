01 / THE LINE

Built, and running

The full first-launch sequence on a 26-second loop. Four things to watch, in the order they happen:

What the loop shows

Words left, number right — always. The left slot holds the message, the right slot holds whatever is counting. Nothing swaps sides at any point in the sequence.
While downloading — “120 of 350 MB” counts up in the right slot, in place, with no crossfade: the digits would blink once a second. No clock — a download’s remaining time is the least trustworthy number the app has, and the megabytes already say how far along it is.
At the seam — the handoff. The 88 pt bar contracts to 46 and stays full while two 18 pt boxes grow beside it.
After the download — the megabytes are spent, so the same right slot swaps them for the clock. First time in the sequence a time appears, and the first time one is backed by a remembered duration.
At ready — the subtext row closes over 0.34 s and the bar rises to meet “Voice ready”. Nothing follows it but the fade, so there is no gap to reserve.

02 / TITLE WEIGHT

How heavy is “higher weight”

Four combinations of the two variables, frozen on the same moment so they can be compared directly. At 600 the title stops being a caption and starts being a heading, which is more presence than a transient status line may want over someone’s book. At 500 it separates from the subtext without asserting itself.

03 / SUBTEXT

How faint is faint

All four running the download state at full tilt — the message cycling on the left, live megabytes on the right. Below about 40% the megabytes stop being reliably legible on the bright ground under the glow’s brightest breath, which is why the right slot is set one weight heavier than the words beside it.

04 / WHAT GOES WHERE

Every phase, in three slots

Two cycling sets, one per half of the wait. The download’s set owns “one-time install” — it is only ever true of a download. The warm-up’s set trades it for “will be faster next time”, which is the equivalent promise for a warm-up and is also literally what expectedWarmUpSeconds is for. Both end on “almost done”, so wherever the cycle happens to land near the finish it lands on something true. Everything else is said once: a retry, a stall, a failure. The clock appears only where there is a real remembered duration behind it.

Two rules the layout needs

Reserve the row mid-flow, collapse it at the end. “Checking the voice” has nothing to say, but something follows it — so the row keeps its height and stays blank, or the bar would jump 15 pt up and straight back down. “Voice ready” has nothing to say and nothing follows it but the fade, so the row closes and the bar rises to meet the title.
The separator only exists when both slots do. One slot alone is centred on its own, with no orphaned dot beside it.

05 / THE SEAM

Handoff, and the last open question

The seam is settled — the full-width download bar contracts to 46 pt and stays full while two 18 pt boxes grow out of the space it gave up. The download becomes step one of three rather than being replaced, which is what warmUpProgressFloor exists to protect. Deal, split, zipper and crossfade are retired.

What is left is how many boxes. Three phases, or the eight stages the loader actually reports? Both below, running the same loop.

Still the one that matters

A failed warm-up shows a green “Voice ready”. KokoroStatusModel.update calls beginReadyBeat() on any transition out of a warming state (KokoroComposition.swift:154), and .unavailable and .removed both have isWarming == false. So a download that failed, a stage load that failed twice, and a model the reader deleted in Storage all end on the green light and the words “Voice ready”. Then the glow fades and the book reads in the system voice with nothing said.

09 / TO DECIDE

Three things, then I build it

  • Title weight — Medium 11 or SemiBold 11. My read is Medium: SemiBold turns a transient status into a heading on a page someone is reading. §02 has both at true scale.
  • Subtext — 9.5 pt at 50% is what §01 runs. §03 has 9 / 9.5 / 10 and 40 / 50 / 60% so you can pick the pair, judged against live megabytes.
  • Three segments or eight — §05 shows both. Three is calmer; eight is what the code actually knows.
